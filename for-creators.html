What happens after someone hits enter.

Two different kinds of AI do two different jobs, at the same time.

A writer reads every clip of yours and drafts the answer. It's good with words, which is also why it can't be trusted to quote you on its own.

A decider, called Jev, never writes a single word. It only picks from options it's given and says how sure it is. It decides what to show this person, in about a fifth of a second.

Then plain code checks the quotes and puts the page together.

How it decides without guessing.

Jev answers multiple-choice questions about what someone typed, and every answer comes with odds. Which topic is this? What are they after?

The page only acts on an answer it's clearly sure of. When it isn't, it doesn't guess. It asks the visitor one quick question, and their tap counts more than anything it worked out.

That's the difference between this and an AI that pretends to know you.

The rule: act only when it's at least 60% sure, and at least 20 points ahead of its second choice. Anything less, it asks.
