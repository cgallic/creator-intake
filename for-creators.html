How it works
Try it

For creators

One page. Every visitor gets a different one.

Four visitors, four pages

Real answers from the live page. Each visitor typed one thing; a decision model read only what that tells it, and the page reshaped itself. Where it wasn't sure, it said so, and asked.

How it decides

Two models with two jobs. A language model writes the answer from the creator's own videos. Jev, a decision model from TypeSafe, never writes a word: it answers typed questions with calibrated odds in about a fifth of a second, and plain code turns those odds into the page.

01 · while the answer is written

Reads the question

Only what one question can really tell you: its topic, and whether they're learning, fixing their own problem, sizing up the product, or want the creator. Plus which of the creator's cards fit.

02 · after

Judges the videos

Every quote is checked word for word against the captions, then Jev checks it's on point. The "watch next" clips come from the same topic, each judged against what the visitor asked.

03 · as they use it

Learns from what they do

Open a clip, tap a card, answer the one-tap question: the page re-reads and reshapes in a quarter second, with no new answer written.

What it won't do

  • Guess who you are. A topic or intent counts only when the model is confident and clearly ahead of the runner-up. Everything else stays unknown.
  • Ask before it answers. The one-tap question comes after the answer, only when it's unsure, and it's optional.
  • Put guesses in your mouth. What it infers changes the layout, never the words of the answer.
  • Quote anyone but the creator. Interviewers, guests, ads and AI voices are screened out of the videos it can quote.
  • Make up a quote. A quote shows only if it appears word for word in the creator's captions, and it plays from that second.
  • Remember you on a server. What it learns about a visitor stays in their own browser.
